How to use
Pitch / Spectrogram — toggle between a pitch tracker and a frequency spectrogram. Both show a 10-second rolling window.
Click & hold on the graph to see the note at your cursor position.
Right-click to pin a reference line at any frequency. Each line gets its own color and appears in both views. Remove lines individually or clear all from the header.
Tone volume slider controls the reference tone loudness during guided exercises.
Spacebar pauses/unpauses the graph so you can analyze what's on screen.
Record your full session or individual exercises for playback.
Voice health & safety
• Pain, burning, or a strained feeling means stop — not push through. Rest beats any streak.
• Hoarseness lasting more than a day or two deserves rest, and more than two weeks deserves an ENT visit.
• Warm up gently, keep sessions short, and let loud moments live in resonance rather than in force.
• This is a self-practice space, not medical care or speech therapy.
Plain-language glossary
Pitch — how high or low your voice sounds.
Resonance — the size and shape of the space the sound vibrates in. The tongue root changes it; the larynx follows along.
Strong / soft voice (chest & head) — the two vocal textures your voice balances between: strong/powerful and soft/light. Fem voice usually wants a blend of both. (Some coaches call related qualities “vocal weight” — a name that invites squeezing, so we avoid it.)
Tongue root — the base of your tongue; moving it up/down drives larynx height and cavity size. Never push the larynx by hand — old forum advice, modern coaches advise against it.
Prosody — the melody of speech: rhythm, stress, and intonation.
Formants — measurable traces of resonance (shown as spectrogram bands; kept in the background until you want them).
